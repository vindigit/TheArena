# Luke Part 1 rendered-motion review

The [actual-game capture](evidence/part1-repeated-transitions.webm) was decoded sequentially and visually inspected after the Luke-only rebuild. The capture uses the real gameplay update, a fixed root-relative camera, and three repeats of walking, ball placement within pickup radius, possession recovery, moving gather, release and stopping. [Recorded gameplay samples](evidence/part1-repeated-transitions.json) establish which simulation states were exercised.

The recording contains **594 decoded rendered frames spanning 69.054 seconds**, while the replay advances gameplay at a fixed 60 Hz. Capture wall time is therefore much slower than simulation time. Nominal simulation times such as 1.5, 5.5 and 9.5 seconds do not locate the gathers in the movie. The inspected gather boundaries occur around **14.26, 43.53 and 58.96 seconds** of capture time. Decoder seeking was unreliable on this file, so evidence extraction used sequential frame order. [Review metadata](evidence/part1-motion-review-metadata.json) records frame indices, actual timestamps and the movie SHA-256.

[Comparable close crops of all three gathers](evidence/part1-motion-review.jpg) show the preceding staggered walking stance settling into the shooting stance. Knees retain a plausible direction, foot spacing does not collapse inward, and the pelvis does not show the former hybrid pop. Small foot settling remains visible as the stance changes. The previous large inward-looking leg change is absent from the reviewed sequences. Separate rendered samples around the repeated pickup windows also retain the moving leg pose without an obvious pelvis drop or sudden leg-ground-correction enable.

The result is a usable procedural baseline, not a finished basketball animation pack. The hands and ball still enter the gathering presentation directly, and the shallow shooting stance remains stiff compared with authored athletic motion. Dribble, pickup and shot presentation should receive dedicated Luke-specific motion and ball-contact work in Part 2. Those visible limitations do not reproduce the diagnosed lower-body collapse. This capture demonstrates pose behavior in the scripted actual-game replay; it is not a measurement of real-time frame rate or physical-phone performance. The isolated rig views, ordinary launch, controls and load-failure checks are separate Part 1 evidence.
